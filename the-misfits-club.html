A warning for anyone taking spirituality too seriously and the realities and demands of the material world too lightly.

Many start out with sound minds and honest questions about God, existence, and enlightenment. Somewhere along the way, the search itself becomes the trap. Concepts multiply, the seeker hardens into an identity, and the very mind that was once clear becomes a misfit in the world it must still live in.

This is a look at how that happens—and where it leads.

1. God as a Concept

“God exists” is a concept.
“God does not exist” is also a concept.

If you approach this as a truth seeker, sooner or later you’re left with one question:
“Why am I holding this concept?”

At some point, if you’re lucky, you discover that even this question is a form of indulgence. No answer comes. Instead, the question itself drops.

When there is no concept left, and no question either, the “you” you have always known yourself to be also drops. What remains is simply the biological functioning of this organism. All it is interested in is survival and reproduction—in plain words, eating and fucking.

It’s fine to live with concepts.
The trouble begins when some minds go too far with non existing spiritual concepts, expecting them to answer tangible, material questions.

That’s where it goes wrong.

They become misfits in society, and then they’re gone. First they enter the Misfits Club, and then the Goners Club.

2. Enlightenment as a Concept

“Enlightenment exists” is a concept.
“Enlightenment does not exist” is also a concept.

It’s fine to live with concepts.

The trouble begins when some minds go too far with this, and are left with little or no energy for the activities needed for their survival and reproduction.

I have seen two kinds of misfits:

One – who is neither in the material world nor in real, serious spiritual practice with consistency and diligence. Instead, they use spirituality as cheap dopamine and a temporary escape from worldly problems and worries.

Two – who has done serious practice, had mystical experiences, and then, carried away by these petty little experiences, spends the rest of life struggling to cope with the material world. Lost in the memory of those experiences and in the craving for them, instead of either surrendering to the silence and stillness, or fully channeling their energy into worldly life—building money, stability, and a support system.

That’s where it goes wrong.

They become misfits in society, and then they’re gone. First they enter the Misfits Club, and then the Goners Club.

3. The Universal Trap

This isn't limited to God or enlightenment. Any idea, when held too tightly, consumes all energy. Nothing remains for eating, working, loving, functioning. The mind clings to it, lives inside it, and the body pays the price. The Misfits Club accepts all kinds of seekers.

Closing Note

If this sting, check where your energy is going: into eating, working, loving, and functioning in the world sanely and intelligently, or living in a fantasy world—singing Loony Tunes and merry melodies, lost in conversations and practices that deliver no tangible returns.

The Misfits Club is always recruiting; it never releases members.

— Written by a resident of the Misfits Club, observing how seekers end up here.
